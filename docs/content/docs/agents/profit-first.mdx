# Protect restock. Allocate the margin.

Customer deposits and wholesale purchase payments are **not** merchant profit. Only seller-declared realised sales and documented costs can form an allocation proposal.

```text
Real revenue = gross sales − direct COGS
Allocatable margin = real revenue − variable fees − refund reserve
Profit + owner's pay + tax reserve + OPEX = allocatable margin
```

The illustrative default is 10% profit, 25% owner's pay, 25% tax reserve, 40% OPEX, calculated on allocatable margin. These are budget examples, not tax or investment advice. Rounding remainder belongs to OPEX; every USDC micro-unit is conserved.

## Contract boundaries

`MercentaProfitVault` is non-upgradeable, limited to Arc Testnet (5042002), using six-decimal USDC at `0x3600000000000000000000000000000000000000`. It has no admin custody, no lending/yield adapter and no access to the Mercenta customer ledger.

A settlement transfers **the seller's wallet USDC** into isolated Restock, Fees, Refund Reserve, Owner's Pay, Tax and OPEX buckets plus a quarterly locked-profit lot. Each caller can withdraw only their own unlocked buckets to their own wallet. Profit can be claimed in bounded batches after the next UTC January/April/July/October boundary.

- Maximum: 10 test USDC per allocation.
- Minimum allocatable-margin floor: 5% of gross.
- Profit budget: 5–30%; all target percentages must sum to 100%.
- Sale IDs are unique per wallet; duplicate settlement reverts.
- Non-positive margin, dust-profit and invalid allocations revert.
- COGS, fees, reserves and sales are seller declarations, not onchain-certified invoices.
- Never send tokens directly: direct token transfers are not attributed or recoverable through an admin rescue.
- Exact gross approval only. Network gas uses native test USDC (18 decimals); the token view uses six decimals. They describe the same asset, not additive balances.

## No automatic Earn

Profit locking does not produce yield. Live Earn is disabled until supported network/vault addresses, reviewed adapter behaviour, loss and liquidity disclosures, accounting, and withdrawal evidence are implemented. A hypothetical APR formula is a scenario, not an available or guaranteed return.
